---
title: Organizations
description: Work with other people in an organization, on one set of connections and agents.
---

Everything in Personal Agent Proxy is in an organization: its connections, each a member's own or
shared by them with others in it, its agents, each shared with everyone in it or one member's own,
its activity log and each member's vault. Everyone
starts with one of their own, and can make more, such as one for work beside a personal one, or
join someone else's. Every page is one organization's, named first in its address
(`/acme/connections`), so a link to a page is a link to it in that organization; the mark at the
top of the sidebar is the one you are in: hover it to switch, to open its **Members** or
**Settings**, or to create another. Your agents there reach what is connected there.

## Your own, or shared

A connection is whoever connected it's, and theirs to share:

- **Just me**: only you and your agents can use it. Nobody else in the organization sees it, its
  owner included. Your mailbox belongs here, and so do your vault and your sandboxes, which are
  yours in every organization you are in and can't be shared.
- **Shared with members by name**: the agents of the members you pick can use it too, with the
  permissions on its page. A mailbox two of you answer belongs here, or a vault for one team
  ([Vault](/connections/vault)).
- **Everyone in the organization**: every member's agents can use it, and so can the shared
  agents. A team account, a shared mailbox or a database user belongs here.

Whoever a connection is shared with sees it listed, with who else has it, never its password or
token, and changes nothing of it. A shared connection acts as the account it was connected with.
For a mailbox, a database, an API key or a custom MCP server that is the account itself; for a
service you sign in to, such as Google, Granola, Notion or Linear, it is whoever signed in, so the
agents of everyone it is shared with work as that person there. The connection's page says who it
acts as and whose it is.

Once anyone else is in the organization, or invited to it, **Add connection** asks whether a new
connection is for you or for everyone, and **Connections** lists what is shared apart from what is
yours alone. On the page of a connection of yours, **Sharing** lists **Everyone in** the
organization and each member: tick one to share it, which asks first, and untick it to take it
back, at once. Whoever loses a connection loses what their agents were set to do with it too. A
connection is renamed, disconnected, signed in to again, shared and has its permissions changed by
whoever's it is; one shared with everyone, by the organization's owner too.

## Agents

An agent login is yours or shared with the organization, the same way a connection is:

- **Just me**: only you see it. It reaches every connection you use, your own and those shared
  with you, with the permissions you give it.
- **Everyone in the organization**: every member sees it and can give its login to their own tools.
  It reaches the connections shared with everyone, never one that is someone's alone or shared
  with members by name, so sharing an agent of yours drops its settings on every other connection. Whoever made it and the organization's owner change
  its access, revoke it, hand it a new [password](/agents/web) and delete it; everyone else sees
  it as it is.

Once anyone else is in the organization, or invited to it, **New agent** asks who the agent is for,
and **External agents and permissions** lists the two apart. On the page of an agent of your own, **Share with everyone**
under **Sharing** shares it, for good. What it did on connections shared with everyone so far
becomes everyone's with it; what it did on any other connection, your own, one shared by name or
one since disconnected, stays yours, along with the tasks it did that for. A login made when an MCP
client signs in is yours. The activity log shows what shared agents did, and what your own agents
did; another member's own agent's doings are theirs, on a connection of yours too. What anyone did
from the home page on a connection shared with everyone is logged with their name.

## Members

**Members**, under the organization's mark at the top of the sidebar, lists who is in the
organization and who is invited. Every organization has one owner, whoever made it, for good;
everyone else is a member. The owner invites people by email, removes members, renames the
organization and deletes it. Members use everything in it and can leave.

An invitation is a link that works for a week, for the address it was sent to: the person signs in,
or creates an account, as that address to join. Inviting follows the same rules as signing up, so
on a Personal Agent Proxy that limits who can sign up, an address it doesn't allow can't be
invited unless it has an account already. Invitations are emailed as sign-in links are, or written to the log until sending email is
set up ([Sign-in](/self-hosting/sign-in#sign-in-links)).

## Leaving and deleting

A member leaves an organization from its **Settings** or **Members**; its owner can't, and deletes
it from its **Settings** instead, as long as it isn't the last one they own, so everyone always owns
one. Your own account is under **Account**, at the foot of the sidebar, with every
organization you are in.
Leaving, or being removed, disconnects what you connected there, what you shared with members by
name included, and deletes your own agents there, and signs out the MCP clients you signed in as
its shared agents. What you shared with everyone stays, the organization's owner's from then on,
and so do the shared agents. The shared agents you made, whose passwords you were shown, get one
nobody knows and are signed out everywhere, until the owner hands them a new one; another shared
agent's password a former member knows keeps working until whoever manages the agent resets it. Deleting an organization disconnects every
connection in it, whoever's it is, and deletes its vaults, every agent and its
activity, for everyone in it. Deleting your account deletes every organization you own, with
everyone else in them losing them too, and takes you out of the others.
