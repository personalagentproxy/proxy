---
title: Organizations
description: Work with other people in an organization, on one set of connections and agents.
---

Everything in Personal Agent Proxy is in an organization: its connections and agents, each shared
with everyone in it or one member's own, its activity log, its vault and each member's own. Everyone
starts with one of their own, and can make more, such as one for work beside a personal one, or
join someone else's. Every page is one organization's, named first in its address
(`/acme/connections`), so a link to a page is a link to it in that organization; the mark at the
top of the sidebar is the one you are in: hover it to switch, or to create another. Your agents
there reach what is connected there.

## Your own or everyone's

A connection is either yours or shared with everyone in the organization:

- **Just me**: only you and your agents can use it. Nobody else in the organization sees it, its
  owner included. Your mailbox belongs here, and so does your vault, which is yours in every
  organization you are in.
- **Everyone in the organization**: every member's agents can use it, with the permissions on its
  page. Members see it listed, never its password or token. A team account, a shared mailbox or a
  database user belongs here, and so does the organization's vault, which every organization has
  from the start ([Vault](/connections/vault)).

A shared connection acts as the account it was connected with. For a mailbox, a database, an API
key or a custom MCP server that is the account itself; for a service you sign in to, such as
Google, Granola, Notion or Linear, it is whoever signed in, so everyone's agents work as that
person there. The connection's page says who it acts as and who connected it.

Once anyone else is in the organization, or invited to it, **Add connection** asks who a new
connection is for, and **Connections** lists the two apart. On the page of a connection of your own,
**Share with everyone** under **Sharing** shares it. Sharing can't be undone: to have it as your own
again, disconnect it and connect it again. A shared connection is renamed, disconnected, signed in
to again and has its permissions changed by whoever connected it and the organization's owner;
everyone else uses it as it is.

## Agents

An agent login is yours or shared with the organization, the same way a connection is:

- **Just me**: only you see it. It reaches the connections shared with everyone and your own, with
  the permissions you give it.
- **Everyone in the organization**: every member sees it and can give its login to their own tools.
  It reaches the connections shared with everyone, never anyone's own, so sharing an agent of yours
  drops its settings on your own connections. Whoever made it and the organization's owner change
  its access, revoke it, hand it a new password and delete it; everyone else sees it as it is.

Once anyone else is in the organization, or invited to it, **New agent** asks who the agent is for,
and **Agents** lists the two apart. On the page of an agent of your own, **Share with everyone**
under **Sharing** shares it, for good; its tasks and activity so far become everyone's with it,
except what it did on your own connections, which stays yours. A login made when an MCP client
signs in is yours. The activity log shows what shared agents did on shared connections, and what
your own agents did; another member's own agent's doings are theirs. What anyone did from the home
page on a shared connection is logged with their name.

## Members

**Members** lists who is in the organization and who is invited. Every organization has one owner,
whoever made it, for good; everyone else is a member. The owner invites people by email, removes
members, renames the organization and deletes it. Members use everything in it and can leave.

An invitation is a link that works for a week, for the address it was sent to: the person signs in,
or creates an account, as that address to join. Inviting follows the same rules as signing up, so
on a Personal Agent Proxy that limits who can sign up, an address it doesn't allow can't be
invited. Invitations are emailed as sign-in links are, or written to the log until sending email is
set up ([Sign-in](/self-hosting/sign-in#sign-in-links)).

## Leaving and deleting

A member leaves an organization from **Members**; its owner can't, and deletes it from
**Settings** instead, as long as it isn't the last one they own, so everyone always owns one.
Leaving, or being removed, disconnects what you connected for yourself there and deletes your own
agents there, and signs out the MCP clients you signed in as its shared agents; the shared
connections and agents stay. A shared agent's password a former member knows keeps working until
whoever manages the agent hands it a new one. Deleting an organization disconnects every
connection in it, shared and every member's own, and deletes its vaults, every agent and its
activity, for everyone in it. Deleting your account deletes every organization you own, with
everyone else in them losing them too, and takes you out of the others.
