---
title: Email
description: Connect a mailbox with an app password, and what to do when the provider will not make one.
---

Personal Agent Proxy connects a mailbox over IMAP and SMTP and signs in to it with an app
password: a password your mail provider makes for one app, apart from the one you sign in with.
Revoking it in your mail account disconnects Personal Agent Proxy and nothing else.

<Steps>

### Make an app password

Follow your provider's steps below. Most show the password once, so keep the page open until it
is in Personal Agent Proxy.

### Connect the mailbox

In Personal Agent Proxy, open **Connections**, then **Add connection**, and **Connect** on Email.
Pick the provider, then enter the mailbox's address and the app password. The spaces some
providers show in it can stay.

### Give agents access

Personal Agent Proxy signs in to the mailbox before saving it, so a wrong password shows straight
away. A new mailbox gives agents nothing: on the connection's page, turn on the actions every agent
gets by default, such as Read, Write drafts and Send, or set them for one agent on the agent's page.

</Steps>

## Gmail

<Steps>

### Turn on 2-Step Verification

Google only makes app passwords for accounts with
[2-Step Verification](https://myaccount.google.com/signinoptions/twosv) on.

### Create the app password

Open [App passwords](https://myaccount.google.com/apppasswords), enter a name such as Personal
Agent Proxy, and **Create**. Google shows a 16-letter password once.

</Steps>

If you are signed in to more than one Google account, check the account in the top right corner
of the page before creating it.

<Callout type="warning">
  Changing your Google password revokes every app password, which disconnects Personal Agent
  Proxy. Make a new one and connect the mailbox again.
</Callout>

### "The setting you are looking for is not available for your account"

Google shows this on the App passwords page when the account cannot make one:

- **2-Step Verification is off.** Turn it on, then open the page again.
- **2-Step Verification uses only security keys.** Add another way to verify, such as an
  authenticator app or your phone.
- **Advanced Protection is on.** Accounts in Google's Advanced Protection Program cannot use app
  passwords.
- **It is a work or school account.** Your Google Workspace admin decides; see below.

### Google Workspace

For a work or school account, these are set by the admin in the
[Google Admin console](https://admin.google.com):

- Under **Security → Authentication → 2-Step Verification**, people need to be allowed to turn
  on 2-Step Verification, and its methods cannot be limited to security keys only.
- Under **Apps → Google Workspace → Gmail → End User Access**, IMAP access needs to be on.

If your admin does not allow app passwords, Personal Agent Proxy cannot connect the mailbox yet.

## iCloud

<Steps>

### Turn on two-factor authentication

Apple only makes app-specific passwords for Apple Accounts with two-factor authentication, and
iCloud Mail needs to be turned on in iCloud settings on one of your devices.

### Create the app password

Sign in at [account.apple.com](https://account.apple.com/account/manage), open **Sign-In and
Security**, then **App-Specific Passwords**, and add one named Personal Agent Proxy.

</Steps>

Connect with your iCloud Mail address, such as `name@icloud.com`. Changing your Apple Account
password revokes app-specific passwords, which disconnects Personal Agent Proxy.

## Fastmail

In Fastmail, open **Settings → Privacy & Security** and create a **New app password**. Give it
access to mail over IMAP and SMTP.

## Yahoo

In your Yahoo account, open **Account security** and **Generate app password**. Name it Personal
Agent Proxy and copy the password it shows.

## Other providers

Choose **Other** to connect any other mailbox, and enter its servers as well:

- **IMAP server**: TLS on port 993.
- **SMTP server**: TLS on port 465, or STARTTLS on 587.

Your provider's help pages list its servers. Use an app password if the provider makes them, and
your mailbox's password if it does not. For Other, the password is used exactly as typed,
spaces included.

## When it does not connect

**"The mailbox turned the password down."** Check that the address is the mailbox the password
was made for. Make a new app password if you are not sure the old one was copied whole.

**"The mail server could not be reached."** With Other, check the servers and the SMTP port. On
your own server, see [Troubleshooting](/self-hosting/troubleshooting#a-mailbox-does-not-connect).

**"This mailbox is already connected."** The address is connected already. Find it under
**Connections**.
